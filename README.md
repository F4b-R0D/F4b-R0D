**MA Economics, University of Manitoba · Winnipeg, Canada**

I am an applied economist working at the intersection of **causal inference, spatial econometrics, infrastructure economics, and regional development**.

My research studies how transportation and connectivity shocks affect **population, employment, accessibility, and regional resilience**, with particular attention to heterogeneous treatment effects, spatial spillovers, and infrastructure dependence in remote and Northern communities.

## Current Research

### Hudson Bay Railway Corridor

**Connectivity Disruptions and Socioeconomic Resilience in Remote Canada**

I study the socioeconomic effects of the **2017 Hudson Bay Railway disruption** across Northern Manitoba.

The project combines Canadian Census data, GIS-based infrastructure exposure, and quasi-experimental methods to analyze how remote communities respond to the temporary loss of critical transportation infrastructure.

**Methods**

`Difference-in-Differences` · `Event Studies` · `Spatial Exposure` · `Moran's I` · `LISA` · `Synthetic Control` · `GIS`

---

### Road Connectivity in Northern Canada

Extension of research on the effects of road connectivity on employment and education in Northern Canada.

The project reconstructs a longitudinal municipality-level panel across multiple Census waves and integrates geographic harmonization, transportation data, GIS, and modern staggered-treatment estimators.

**Methods**

`TWFE` · `Staggered DiD` · `Callaway & Sant'Anna` · `GTFS` · `GIS Buffers` · `Network Exposure`

## Research Question

> **How do infrastructure and connectivity shocks reshape economic activity, population dynamics, and resilience across space?**

I am particularly interested in settings where standard counterfactual construction is difficult because communities differ sharply in geography, accessibility, industrial structure, population size, and exposure to infrastructure networks.
